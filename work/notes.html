Design notes

One portfolio. A few ways in.

Some thoughts behind the website I'm building, from a plain page to a world with a character of its own.

Dimension choices Interface over effects A personal avatar

Dimension choices

My starting idea was to let visitors choose how they explore the portfolio. The names 0D, 1D, 2D, and 3D describe the visual experiences I'm imagining.

  • 0D: a direct page of text and images. The work is the focus.
  • 1D: a guided path through the content, with one clear sequence to follow.
  • 2D: a pixel-art world or a playful desktop that opens up the projects.
  • 3D: a scene with a personal character and small, responsive interactions.

0D, 1D, 2D, and the 3D experiment are available now. Visitors can choose a mode directly, without having to complete the others first.

Interface over effects

I like the idea of a website that surprises people. But a portfolio also needs to answer simple questions: what is the work, what was the idea, and how can someone get in touch?

That is why the 0D version comes first. It gives the content a clear home. The other versions can build on it with movement, characters, and exploration while keeping the important information easy to find.

One transition idea is to let a plain page become a line, the line unfold into a pixel world, and that world gain depth. It is a direction to explore for the future modes, rather than an effect built into this page.

A personal avatar

The 3D character should feel connected to me, rather than being a generic mascot. My photographs are the reference for its appearance; the result can still be stylized.

Small reactions are part of the idea: looking toward the pointer, blinking, smiling, or shaking its head when an eye is clicked. The character should add personality while leaving the projects readable.

The current avatar is an early experiment. I'm pausing its appearance changes while I build out the simpler version of the portfolio.

Design notes

One portfolio. A few ways in.

Some thoughts behind the website I'm building, from a plain page to a world with a character of its own.

Dimension choices Interface over effects A personal avatar

Dimension choices

My starting idea was to let visitors choose how they explore the portfolio. The names 1D, 2D, and 3D describe the visual experiences I'm imagining.

  • 1D: a direct page of text and images. The work is the focus.
  • 2D: a guided path through the content, with one clear sequence to follow.
  • 3D: a pixel-art world or a playful desktop that opens up the projects.

1D, 2D, 3D are available now. Visitors can choose a mode directly, without having to complete the others first.

Interface over effects

I like the idea of a website that surprises people. But a portfolio also needs to answer simple questions: what is the work, what was the idea, and how can someone get in touch?

That is why the 1D version comes first. It gives the content a clear home. The other versions can build on it with movement, characters, and exploration while keeping the important information easy to find.

One transition idea is to let a plain page become a line, the line unfold into a pixel world, and that world gain depth. It is a direction to explore for the future modes, rather than an effect built into this page.

A personal avatar

The pixel companion should feel connected to me. My photographs are the reference for its appearance; the result can still be stylized.

Small reactions are part of the idea: waving hello, walking to the arcade, and exploring the room between games. The character should add personality while leaving the projects readable.

The companion adds a familiar face to the desktop. The projects and contact details stay easy to reach through the app windows.
